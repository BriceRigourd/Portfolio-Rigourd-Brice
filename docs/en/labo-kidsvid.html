The idea

Produce YouTube Kids videos end to end with no budget: narrated bedtime stories and sung nursery rhymes, in French and English from a single script, using only open-source tools running on my PC.

What I built

The pipeline

From a one-sentence idea to an MP4 ready to publish

n8n orchestrates, the API executes. Every step can resume: if its output already exists, it is skipped, so a crash in the middle of a batch of images loses nothing.

  1. 01Ollama + Qwen3

    Script

    Checked automatically: banned words, brands, length. Up to three rewrites before it is rejected.

  2. 02Piper

    Voice

    One track per scene: durations are exact, so the subtitles line up without any transcription.

  3. 03SDXL via ComfyUI

    Illustrations

    A fixed style, the characters' physical description re-injected word for word and a stable seed, to keep the same faces from one image to the next.

  4. 04ACE-Step 1.5

    Music

    An instrumental bed for the stories, or a nursery rhyme sung in French.

  5. 05FFmpeg

    Editing

    A Ken Burns effect per scene, crossfades, and music ducked automatically under the voice.

  6. 06Pillow + LLM

    Packaging

    A thumbnail taken from a frame of the video, readable at 120 px wide; title, description and tags written with no brands and no clickbait.

  7. 07FFmpeg + Python

    Quality control

    Loudness, silences, metadata and similarity to the previous episodes.

  8. 08Human

    Review, then YouTube

    A human reviews every video, then the publishing workflow uploads it as private. It only goes public after a check.

Bilingual: one route creates the English version from the French script, with the same scenes. One story gives two videos from a single set of images.

Three n8n workflows: narrated story (16 nodes), sung nursery rhyme (12 nodes) and YouTube publishing (14 nodes), annotated on the canvas.

The channel bible

The file that separates a channel from a content farm

Characters, world, tone and rituals are re-injected into every prompt. Here is the example shipped with the pipeline (its English version), meant to be replaced by a real bible:

Nino's Clearing

Gentle bedtime stories that help 3-to-6-year-olds put words to what they feel, told through a fox cub who is learning right along with them.

  • Ninothe hero, a curious and slightly shy 4-year-old fox cub
  • Pistachehis practical best friend, a hedgehog
  • Granny Bearthe reassuring figure, who asks questions instead of giving answers

Rituals: every episode opens on the clearing with the same stream sound, and ends with Nino settling into the stump and letting out a big breath.

Automatic guardrails

Checked on every episode, before my own review.

−14 LUFS

YouTube's target loudness, with an alert above −11 or below −20.

82%

Similarity threshold against the 25 previous episodes: above it, the angle has to change.

3 s

The longest silence allowed in the final track.

6 / day

Upload cap: 10,000 quota units per day, 1,600 per video.

The script is filtered (violence, fear, brands and licensed characters, clickbait) and the “Made for Kids” setting is forced: otherwise, quality control blocks publication.

What this project taught me

A

Read the licenses before the benchmarks

The best free models are often barred from commercial use. Ruling them out from the start avoids redoing everything on the day the channel needs to earn money.

B

The rule that decides everything

Since July 2025, YouTube speaks of “inauthentic content” and judges at the channel level: thirty interchangeable episodes can get the whole channel demonetized at once.

C

The economics of kids' content

“Made for Kids” is a legal requirement (COPPA): no personalized ads, no comments, no notifications. Revenue per view is low, so everything rests on volume and on how long the videos keep being watched.

D

Visual consistency has to be built

A fixed style, a re-injected description and a stable seed give a baseline. For a truly identical character, you need a LoRA, or IP-Adapter as a shortcut.

E

Even a public-domain tune can be claimed

“Frère Jacques” is in the public domain, but the hundreds of arrangements registered in Content ID are not. So the nursery-rhyme prompt forbids any cover or pastiche.

F

The bottleneck is the writing

Once the stack is installed, a video takes a single command. Quality no longer comes from the technology, but from the channel bible and a human eye before publication.
