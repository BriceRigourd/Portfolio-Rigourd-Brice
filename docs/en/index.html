Summary

ESTIA engineer with a Master's in Smart Grids from the University of the Basque Country (UPV/EHU). He writes the software that replaces manual work for technical teams: tools that read thousands of PDFs, desktop applications, Excel/VBA tools and AI prototypes. He has delivered tools to Amarenco, to Energiter and to a medical-device manufacturer. Orders of magnitude: 4,128 PVsyst reports retrieved and analyzed automatically; technical database updates and checks against SharePoint cut from 2 to 3 weeks to under 10 minutes (under 5 minutes for a simple check, 30 minutes to 1 h 20 for the very first download of a portfolio of more than 300 plants), a tool validated and used from time to time by 3 fellow engineers; 287 PV plants in operation under his supervision at the end of the assignment (14 SPVs, 67.3 MWp, mostly rooftop); over 230 PVsyst studies with discrepancies against the monitoring configuration identified and reported, over a hundred of them being redone; over 40 PV feasibility and yield studies; 2,150 grid access contracts read and checked; about 18,000 lines of Python in the final versions of the Amarenco tools; about 4,750 lines of VBA in PlasmaCraft, delivered with its user manual.

Professional experience and projects

  1. Amarenco, Toulouse, solar power producer. January to December 2025. Consulting engineer in photovoltaic operations (plant performance, monthly reports to shareholders, KPIs, OPEX budget, Asset Management and O&M contracts) across 287 plants at the end of the assignment (14 SPVs, 67.3 MWp, mostly rooftop) and author of the team's automation tools. Project (July to September 2025): a PVsyst orchestrator, his first Python application with a graphical interface, built to keep the internal technical database up to date with the new PVsyst studies uploaded to SharePoint every week by internal and external design offices. Read-only SharePoint connection, download of the latest PVsyst studies of each SPV into a local database with an archive of older versions, extraction, comparison with the internal technical database and the comparison file (missing or outdated data by SPV and project code), project status checks (cancelled, renamed, merged SPVs), three Excel control workbooks; routine updates in 1 to 10 minutes instead of 2 to 3 weeks, simple checks in 10 seconds to 5 minutes, first full download in 30 minutes to 1 h 20 (1 to 10 minutes per SPV); validated and used from time to time by 3 fellow engineers, then handed by the department to a development team for internal deployment. PySide6 and QML Windows application (interface V14.5, orchestrator V12.4); multiprocess extraction of PVsyst v6 to v8 reports in French and English (pdfplumber, pypdf, pdftotext); batch analyses: 26,809 DC/AC ratios extracted and 2,051 plants sorted into six clipping-risk classes, 2,150 grid access contracts read with the latest version kept. PVsyst ↔ SynaptiQ quality control (March to June 2025): asyncio client for the SynaptiQ API (JWT authentication, exponential back-off, TTL cache); about 154,000 SharePoint documents inventoried across 4,660 projects, about 7,500 PVsyst files (latest version kept), 970 active plants checked against their PVsyst (tilt/azimuth pairs identical or within ±1° and ±10°, modules per pair, five losses); over 230 PVsyst studies with discrepancies reported to the teams, over a hundred being redone; mostly orientation discrepancies (azimuth in the wrong cardinal direction) and module counts per orientation. Quality: typed package, mypy --strict, ruff, black, pytest. Feedback from a user at Amarenco (translated from French): "an absolutely brilliant tool".
  2. Energiter, Montpellier, wind and solar engineering consultancy. April to September 2024. Renewable energy design engineer, final-year internship. Project: a grid connection study tool, the subject of his Master's thesis. Download and archiving of Caparéseau (RTE) capacities and S3REnR regional schemes; real road distance through the IGN routing API called asynchronously; analysis of source substations with and without reinforcement works; costing in 17 MW modules, cable chosen by power and distance, network cost and regional cost share; deliverables as KML for Google Earth, folium maps, a project database and a PDF report; Excel/VBA interface driving Python scripts through PowerShell. Over 3,400 source substations filtered by downstream voltage (20 kV or more) and ranked by a 0-to-5 criticality score; asynchronous calls to the IGN API, about 64% less response time. Parallel studies: over 40 PV feasibility and yield studies (MV-connected plants from 2 to 22 MWp, PVsyst with Solargis data), over 15 wind simulations in WAsP and windPRO (3 to 15 MW wind farms, ERA5, MERRA), grid connection studies for RTE and Enedis substations, and a windPRO bat-curtailment method validated against the in-house Python tool (about one point of difference on two wind farms). The project page includes an interactive simulation of the tool on fictitious data.
  3. Ezio Industrial Consulting, Biarritz. May to August 2023. Designer-developer, second-year engineering internship, for a medical-device manufacturer (cold-plasma sterilization). Project: PlasmaCraft, Excel/VBA software for authoring the machine's PLC recipes, designed from the requirements to the user manual. 53 VBA modules, about 4,750 lines, 115 procedures; recipes of up to 100 sequences with four parameters controlled over time; input checks against the machine's limits and step size (0 to 3,600 s per sequence, 0 to 500 W, 0 to 5 sccm, 0 to 22 G); ramps split into 5 to 25 steps that add up exactly; batch editing; TXT export for the PLC and locked XLSM recipes; all nine client requirements covered; a 33-page user manual and an installation guide; name and logo designed and approved by the client.

Personal projects (Lab)

  1. Combat agent trained in simulation, with AION 2 as the test bed. October 2026, in progress. An agent that reads the game screen (640×360 capture at 7 frames per second, detection in about 3 ms without a GPU) and decides every action. Imitation learning on 1,864 examples from 8 play sessions (39.4% accuracy against 21.6% for the majority class, movement-key F1 of 0.62). A 2.5D arena calibrated on the real game, then training of 32 settings with the cross-entropy method: validation score from 16.9 to 19.2 out of 20, and from 15.9 to 18.5 with an unkillable boss. PySide6 Windows application, about 29,000 lines of Python and 740 pytest tests. External approach only, simulation mode by default.
  2. Pulse, a dating app. Since January 2026, in progress. Full-stack Android app: Expo SDK 54, React Native 0.81 and TypeScript; FastAPI and WebSocket server; Supabase database migrated from MongoDB; photos on S3 storage through presigned URLs, with a hand-written SigV4 signature. About 70 API routes, about 16,800 lines of TypeScript and 5,400 lines of server code, 135 commits, releases 1.006 to 1.014. Features: discovery and matching, "Send a Vibe" Super Like, transparent profile, messaging and moderation, Google or email sign-in, four languages. Prototype built with the Emergent agent, then developed with Claude and Claude Code.
  3. Raspberry Pi Pico 2 W as a USB mouse and keyboard. October 2026, completed. Running CircuitPython, the microcontroller poses as a USB mouse and keyboard (HID), receives commands from a Python script over a serial port and draws a scene in Paint (house, tree, sun, star) before typing the name of each drawing. Closed-loop control from the cursor's real position, Paint buttons located through UI Automation, an AZERTY keyboard table, safety guards and a study of USB descriptors. About 680 lines of Python.
  4. KidsVid, AI-generated videos for children, made locally. September 2026, abandoned. A 100% local, open-source pipeline for YouTube Kids: narrated stories and nursery rhymes, in French and English from a single script; n8n orchestrates a FastAPI service chaining Ollama and Qwen3 (writing), Piper (voice), SDXL through ComfyUI (images), ACE-Step (music), FFmpeg (editing), thumbnail, metadata and quality control. About 2,100 lines of Python, 19 API routes, 3 n8n workflows. Lessons: licenses compatible with commercial use, YouTube's July 2025 "inauthentic content" policy, COPPA obligations for "Made for Kids" content.
